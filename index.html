The Mystery of the Missing Fruits

Why do some flowers develop seeds while others do not?

1. The mystery

Two groups of the same type of flowering plant receive the same light, water, temperature and mineral nutrients. Your task: investigate whether pollen reaching the flower makes a difference to seed production.

You are the scientist. Decide how to treat each flower, speed up time, and compare the results. An experiment needs a fair comparison.

What have you discovered?

Pollen grains must reach a compatible stigma for pollination to occur. Pollination can lead to fertilisation, after which ovules develop into seeds. Flowers that receive no pollen normally cannot form seeds through sexual reproduction.

Important distinction: Pollination is not the same as fertilisation, and fruit formation alone is not proof of fertilisation. Some plant species can form seedless fruits.

Think further: How does pollen reach the stigma in nature?

Teacher note: Simulated counts illustrate a testable relationship, not actual tomato data. This model deliberately focuses on sexual seed production in a flowering plant. For a stronger investigation, students can reset and repeat trials, increase flower numbers in a real experiment, and discuss the limitations of deterministic simulations.
